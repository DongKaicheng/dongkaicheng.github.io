01 / Principles

Manifesto

I use AI throughout mathematical research: to explore conjectures, compare arguments, organize theory, and improve exposition. I believe its deepest promise is to make ambitious, sustained theory building more feasible, giving new concepts, foundational structures, and unexpected connections room to develop.

I want this work to be open while it develops. Conjectures, partial results, obstacles, and proofs can all help others move forward. Inspired by the Stacks Project, I aim to share precise statements, readable proofs, traceable dependencies, and a public history of revisions. Mathematical claims should be assessed through their arguments and evidence; AI use should be explicit, and contributions should receive credit. The tools and projects below put these principles into practice.

03 / Projects

Research websites

  • Conjecture Atlas ↗

    An open home for mathematical foundations and long-term research programmes, bringing together definitions, proofs, conjectures, and revision histories.

  • Non-Archimedean Bedford–Taylor Theory ↗

    A research programme within the Atlas developing intrinsic differential operations and pluripotential theory on Berkovich spaces, with conjectures, progress reports, and papers.

  • Beilinson–Bloch Heights and the Strong Green Conjecture ↗

    A research programme on the strong non-Archimedean Green-form conjecture and the construction of Beilinson–Bloch height pairings for homologically trivial cycles.
